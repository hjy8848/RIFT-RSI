## Research question

RIFT asks whether a Designer can learn to propose training packages that improve a Service on *other* tasks. A package receives credit only after an actual Service RL update: the mean change in log probability assigned to demonstrated actions in a frozen, independent replay set. This is a package-level behavioral proxy, not a success rate, causal proof, or a guarantee that a single task caused the gain. Updates must preserve provenance and control training budget, checkpoint, replay snapshot, and comparisons. Historical checkpoints create nonstationary rewards: do not pool them as comparable without an explicitly justified normalization or matched grouping.

Service receives only verified final business outcome (success/failure; unknown is excluded), never strict process success. Designer receives the replay proxy bound to the actual completed Service update and its package; invalid, unverified, or untrained packages have no fabricated reward. Difficulty, failure rate, and generated-task success are not learning value: hard tasks may be unlearnable and easy ones may transfer. Demonstrated actions are behavior observed on a successful execution, not per-step optimality labels; successful episodes can contain unnecessary or poor actions. Replay verification and business outcome are necessary but do not prove each action is good.

Scoring requires both real before and after weights and identical context, tokenization, action tokens and mask. The first version may exercise math with fake scores but cannot produce a learning result, because it does not train either model. The raw sum corresponds to sequence log probability; length-normalized scoring is an explicit alternative, not the same estimator. Results must record the choice. A replay set may induce imitation, length, selection, distribution and reward-hacking biases; held-out likelihood gain is related and should be an explicit baseline. Independent real rollouts remain the final measure of transfer, regression, and safety.

## Isolation and Usami

The Host may inspect private worlds, certificates, Reference witnesses and Oracle evaluations; Service and Designer receive only allowlisted model-visible messages, tool results, public certification status and the approved business outcome. Public tool errors are observations, not Oracle annotations. Do not feed raw native records, private IDs/evidence, strict success, process diagnostics, or task-selection decisions based on those diagnostics to learning. Provenance needed for research must be separated from model inputs. The benchmark's `usami_v2.evaluation.outcomes.OutcomeV2.business_goal_success` is distinct from `strict_task_success`; the former is usable only when evidence is replay-verified and its semantics match the approved outcome. Other native scores cannot be presumed equivalent. Unknown remains unknown.

At this pinned revision, `usami_benchmark.episodes.run/replay` compile verified tasks and replay exact native executions, but their aggregated `task_success` includes process or safety checks; it is not a substitute for business-only reward. `usami_v2` separates business and strict outcomes, but its execution and certification interfaces need explicit adaptation and validation. Certificate reachability is bounded and may be unresolved; a proposed task is not certified merely because it parses. Replay selection based on strict process success would leak privileged process supervision indirectly, so only business outcome and execution-integrity verification can gate examples.

## Relation to prior work

[R-Zero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05004) trains challenger and solver with a difficulty/uncertainty-oriented curriculum. [Teacher-Student Curriculum Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00183) uses student learning progress, and [Learning to Teach](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.03643) optimizes a teacher from student feedback. RIFT shares their teaching objective and is close to held-out validation likelihood improvement and data-selection/gradient-influence approaches. [Learning to Reweight Examples](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09050) uses validation loss and meta-gradients to weight data, while [influence functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04730) estimate training-example effects on predictions. Its proposed distinction is a post-update, decision-context replay signal for generated task packages; independent benefit and novelty are empirical questions, not established by having two agents.

## Required experiments and first-stage scope

Test whether the proxy predicts independent task success and whether optimizing it beats matched random, difficulty, learning-progress, and held-out likelihood teachers under equal rollout, update and scoring budgets. Ablate decision-level versus ordinary validation likelihood, replay composition, token normalization, stale checkpoints, task overlap, forgetting and noise; report uncertainty and independent validation/test rollouts. Replay feedback is a training signal and must not be reused as final test evidence. Partition and deduplicate by world, task, family, and lineage; enforcing all four is conservative and may make data scarce.

The minimal system needs trusted task grounding/certification and exact execution replay, a public trajectory boundary, final-outcome reward, provenance-aware frozen replay, matched checkpoint scorer, two full-parameter RL backends, and a resumable orchestrator. Stage one defines contracts and CPU tests only; without actual full-parameter updates, real scoring, reliable business-outcome adapter, and independent rollouts, it is not a complete RSI system and makes no capability claim. Partial interfaces must fail closed rather than infer success or imitate training.
